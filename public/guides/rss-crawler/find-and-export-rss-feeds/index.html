RSS Crawler guide

Find and Export RSS Feeds from Websites

Scan one website or a larger source list, review validated RSS and Atom endpoints, and export only the feeds you want to keep.

Published July 28, 2026 · Windows

A website may advertise its feed clearly, hide it in page markup, expose it at a common path, or provide several feeds for different sections. RSS Crawler checks multiple discovery routes and keeps the candidates in one reviewable workspace.

1. Prepare the website input

Paste website addresses or feed URLs separated by lines, commas, or spaces. Remove unrelated text and duplicate domains before starting a large scan.

2. Choose the scan depth

Use a quick scan for obvious endpoints, a standard scan for normal discovery, or a deeper scan when a site has a more complex structure. Deeper scans take longer and may produce more candidates to review.

3. Review the discovered feeds

Inspect feed title, format, latest activity, discovery method, and any problem state. Ignore feeds that are stale, irrelevant, duplicated, or tied to the wrong section of the site.

4. Verify content in the Reader

Preview recent items to confirm that a technically valid feed contains the content you expected. Save useful groups as packs when you expect to return to the same source set.

5. Export the clean list

Export included feeds as OPML, CSV, JSON, or copied URLs depending on the destination. Ignored feeds should stay out of the Reader and export set.

Common mistakes

  • Running a deep crawl before trying a faster scan.
  • Keeping every technically valid endpoint.
  • Failing to preview recent items.
  • Exporting duplicate section and site-wide feeds without deciding which one is needed.
